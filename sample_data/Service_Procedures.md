# Asteron Equipment — Service Procedures

**Document ID:** AST-SVC-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective date:** 2026-01-01

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure explains how an owner or operator reports a fault and how Asteron Equipment's service team reviews a service request. It applies to the fictional AX-100 Standard Industrial Power Unit and AX-200 Heavy-Duty Industrial Power Unit.

The procedure supports the warranty review described in **AST-WAR-001, Product Warranty Policy**. Submitting a service request begins a review; it does not establish warranty eligibility or authorize a warranty repair.

## 2. Report a fault

When a fault is observed, the reporter should provide a clear account of what happened and identify the affected unit. Include the product model and serial number, a description of the symptom or damage, and when and under what operating circumstances it was noticed.

The reporter should also preserve and provide relevant records that are available, such as shipment or commissioning information, warranty registration records, maintenance history, and prior service notes. These records help the service team understand the unit's history and assess the applicable warranty conditions.

Reports should distinguish observed facts from assumptions. For example, describe an unusual sound, alarm, or loss of output directly; do not label its cause as a manufacturing defect unless that cause has been established through review.

## 3. Open a service request

To open a service request, contact the Asteron Equipment service team through the designated service channel and provide the information below. The service team records the submission as a request for review.

1. **Product model:** AX-100 or AX-200.
2. **Serial number:** The identifier shown on the unit or its product records.
3. **Fault description:** The observed symptoms, damage, or operating issue, including when it began and whether it is ongoing.
4. **Relevant warranty records:** Available shipment date, commissioning date, warranty or extension registration information, and other records that may establish the applicable coverage period.
5. **Relevant service and maintenance information:** Prior repairs, required maintenance records, and known handling or modification history when related to the reported fault.
6. **Reporter details:** A contact name and a way for the service team to request clarification about the submission.

Provide copies or references to available records rather than changing or recreating them. If a requested record is not available, identify that gap so the service team can determine what clarification is needed.

## 4. Service team review

The service team reviews the submitted information to understand the fault and determine whether the record is complete enough for the next step. The review may include:

1. Confirming that the model and serial number identify the unit under review.
2. Comparing shipment and commissioning records with the standard warranty periods in **AST-WAR-001 §3**.
3. Reviewing the fault description to assess whether it is consistent with a manufacturing defect under normal, authorized operation.
4. Considering relevant handling, modification, and maintenance information against the exclusions in **AST-WAR-001 §4.2**.
5. Reviewing purchase and registration records if an extended warranty is being relied on, using **AST-EW-001, Extended Warranty Terms**.

The review is based on the information available. The service team may ask for more information when dates, fault details, or relevant records are incomplete or inconsistent. A submitted request, acknowledgment, or request for clarification is not a coverage decision.

## 5. Warranty repair authorization

Do not begin or represent a repair as an Asteron-authorized warranty repair until the service team has reviewed the request and provided authorization for that warranty repair. Authorization applies to the repair scope communicated by the service team. If the observed fault changes or additional work appears necessary, submit the new information for review before treating additional work as authorized under the warranty.

This procedure does not establish that every reported fault will qualify for warranty coverage. Eligibility is assessed under the applicable policy and depends on sufficient facts. A service request may be reviewed even when the eventual result is that the fault is outside the stated coverage.

## 6. Incomplete information and escalation

If required information is missing, the service team identifies the open question and requests the relevant detail or record. The request should make clear which unit, date, fault, or warranty evidence needs clarification. The reporter should provide the available information and explain when a record cannot be obtained.

If the missing information prevents the team from confirming the applicable warranty period or evaluating the fault, the request remains unresolved for eligibility purposes until the gap is addressed. The service team may escalate an unresolved or conflicting record for further review. Escalation does not presume approval or denial.

Examples of issues to clarify include:

1. A shipment date that is absent or differs between records.
2. A commissioning date that has not been recorded.
3. A fault description that does not explain what was observed or when it occurred.
4. A maintenance or modification history that is relevant to a possible policy exclusion but is not yet established.
5. A claimed extended warranty for which purchase or registration evidence is incomplete.

## 7. Request record and follow-up

The service request should retain the information supplied, relevant records or references, questions raised during review, and the service team's authorization or eligibility communication. Keeping these items together helps the reporter and reviewer refer to the same product and facts during follow-up.

When responding to a request for additional information, refer to the service request and answer the identified questions. If new evidence changes an earlier understanding of the dates or fault, provide it for review rather than assuming the prior assessment still applies.
